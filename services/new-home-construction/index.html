How it goes

How we build a new home

  1. 1

    Plan & permit

    Drawings and engineering settled, permits filed, budget agreed before a shovel moves.

    We start with how you live, not with a floor plan. From that we develop drawings with an architect or designer, have the structure engineered, and price the house line by line against those drawings — so the number you agree to is for the house you are actually getting. Permits go in with stamped plans, and we handle the zoning, stormwater and utility applications that come with a new lot. Nothing is dug until the budget, the drawings and the permit all say the same thing.

  2. 2

    Site & foundation

    Lot cleared, excavation to the drawings, footings below frost line and walls up square.

    The lot is cleared and graded, and the building corners are staked and checked by a surveyor. We excavate to the drawings, compact a stone base, and pour footings below the frost line sized to the engineer's loads. Foundation walls — poured or precast — go up square and level, get waterproofed and drained, and are backfilled only once the first floor is framed and bracing them. Underground plumbing and sleeves for utilities go in before the slab.

  3. 3

    Framing

    Floors, walls and roof structure go up a storey at a time, inspected as we go.

    Floor systems, walls and roof go up a level at a time, with the framing inspection at each stage before the next starts. Openings are headered to the plans, bearing walls land on bearing below, and everything is strapped and anchored for the wind and snow loads this county sees. Windows and exterior doors are sized and ordered during framing so they arrive the week the rough openings are ready.

  4. 4

    Close in & rough-in

    Roof, windows and doors seal the shell; plumbing, electrical and HVAC run before anything is covered.

    Roofing, windows, doors and house wrap turn the frame into a dry shell. Inside it, the plumber, electrician and HVAC installer run everything — supplies, drains, vents, panel and circuits, ductwork or mini-splits — while the walls are open. Each system is inspected and signed off. This is also when low-voltage, speakers, security and anything else that lives in the walls gets run, because after this it costs a wall to add.

  5. 5

    Insulate & board

    Insulated, vapour-sealed and drywalled — the house becomes rooms.

    Insulation to code or better, air-sealed and with the right vapour control for each wall, then drywall hung, taped and sanded. It is the stage where the building stops being a structure and starts being rooms, and where we walk it with you to confirm switch, outlet and fixture positions before paint.

  6. 6

    Finish & hand over

    Floors, kitchen, baths, trim, paint. Punch list closed, keys handed over.

    Flooring, cabinets, counters, tile, interior doors, trim and paint, then plumbing and electrical fixtures set and tested. Exterior work — siding, porches, concrete, landscaping — runs alongside. We do a full punch-list walk with you, close every item, pass the final inspection and hand over keys, manuals, warranties and the as-built drawings.
