How it goes

How we finish a basement

  1. 1

    Moisture & egress check

    Walls, slab and drainage assessed; egress windows planned where bedrooms are going.

    Before any framing, we find out whether the basement is dry enough to finish. Walls and slab are checked for seepage and efflorescence, grading and gutters outside are looked at, and sump and drainage are assessed. If there is a moisture problem it is fixed first — interior drain, sump, sealing — because finishes over a wet wall fail. If a bedroom is planned, we design an egress window so it is legal and safe.

  2. 2

    Layout & permits

    Rooms laid out around the structure and services that can't move. Permit filed.

    The layout works around what cannot move: the stair, the furnace and water heater, the main beam and columns, the drain stack. We plan bedrooms, bath, living space and storage inside those constraints, keep ceiling height where it counts, and file for the permit with the layout and electrical plan. Headroom, egress and smoke/CO detection are all checked against code at this stage.

  3. 3

    Framing & rough-in

    Stud walls up, drain lines cut into the slab where a bathroom needs them, electrical and HVAC run.

    Walls are framed off the block with a gap and pressure-treated plates, soffits box in ducts and beams, and the ceiling is kept as high as the mechanicals allow. If a bathroom is going in, the slab is cut and drain lines are set and tied to the sewer or an ejector pit. Electrical circuits, lighting, outlets, and heating and cooling for the new rooms are run and inspected.

  4. 4

    Insulate & board

    Insulated against the block, vapour-controlled, drywalled and taped.

    Rigid foam or spray foam against the foundation walls controls moisture and temperature; the stud bays are insulated and the assembly is detailed so it can dry. Drywall is hung, taped and sanded, with moisture-resistant board where it belongs. Access panels are left at shut-offs and cleanouts so the house stays serviceable.

  5. 5

    Bathroom & floors

    Tile, tub or shower, vanity; flooring run wall to wall.

    The bathroom is waterproofed, tiled and fitted — shower or tub, vanity, toilet, exhaust fan vented outside. Flooring suited to below-grade — luxury vinyl plank, engineered wood over a proper underlayment, or tile — is run wall to wall. Doors, closets and built-in storage are installed.

  6. 6

    Light, paint, hand over

    Recessed lighting, paint, doors and trim. A floor you'd never call a basement.

    Recessed lighting laid out for even light with no dark corners, paint, trim and hardware, then a clean. The result is a floor of the house you would never describe as a basement: proper ceiling height, dry, bright and warm, with the inspections and permit closed so it counts as living space when you sell.
